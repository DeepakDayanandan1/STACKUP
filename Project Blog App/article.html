The Architecture of Silence: Designing Spaces That Breathe

Nora Thielemann Jul 28, 2026 · 6 min read

The space between objects

In Japanese aesthetics, ma (間) refers not to space itself but to the interval — the pregnant pause between sound and silence, between wall and window. For centuries it shaped how temples breathed, how gardens arranged stone.

From shoji screens to UI grids

When Dieter Rams formulated his ten principles, "as little design as possible" was the last and arguably most important. Not zero design — as little as the problem demands. There is craftsmanship in restraint that is harder to execute than abundance.

The challenge for contemporary designers is resisting the accumulation of features, colors, and animations that signal effort rather than produce clarity. A well-placed margin is invisible; a cluttered margin announces itself as a failure.

Negative space as intention

When Tadao Ando builds a church from concrete and light, the material recedes and the interval becomes the subject. This is not minimalism as aesthetic preference — it is a rigorous philosophy about what deserves attention.
